Accuracy matters when a reader is planning an electrical system, comparing costs, or making a safety decision.

To report a potential error, include the page URL, the statement in question, and any relevant source or context through our [contact page](/pages/contact.html). We review reports and update content when a correction or clarification is warranted.

Updates may clarify assumptions, repair links, refresh time-sensitive information, or correct factual errors. We do not represent an article as reviewed by a named specialist unless that review is real and disclosed.


## Correction log

- **2026-09-06 — Privacy page did not match reality.** Google Analytics had been loading site-wide without being disclosed in the privacy policy (which covered only Rybbit), and the affiliate-disclosure page stated we "run display advertising (Google AdSense)" when no ads run. Fixed by removing Google Analytics entirely (Rybbit, which is cookieless, remains), rewriting the privacy policy to match actual practices, and correcting the disclosure page. If analytics are ever re-added, they will be disclosed here and on the privacy page first.
- **2026-09-06 — Site search was not functioning.** /search.html rendered an empty article (the search template was never wired to the page), so the nav "Search" item led nowhere useful. The search page now renders the search interface and matches against titles, descriptions, keywords, and page body text.
- **2026-09-06 — MPPT price-band corrections (roundup, reviews, cost guide).** The Victron SmartSolar 100/20 and 100/30 were described as sitting in the "$250–$600 mid-range" MPPT band. Street prices checked September 2026 (official EUR price list, an authorized distributor, and multiple US retailers) put them at ~$95–$125 and ~$110–$140 — squarely in the small class, whose floor was also corrected from $120 to $95. Verified editorial bands were added to the roundup and comparison tables.
- **2026-09-05 — Federal tax-credit status (multiple pages).** Several cost and financing pages still described the 30% federal residential clean-energy credit as available in 2026 ("remains at 30% through 2032"). The credit expired December 31, 2025 under P.L. 119-21. Affected pages were corrected and savings/payback tables recomputed without the credit. If you spot a remaining stale claim, please report it.
- **2026-09-05 — Duplicate cost guides consolidated.** `/guides/solar-battery-cost-2026/` and `/pages/solar-panel-cost-california.html` duplicated near-identical pages elsewhere on the site and now point canonical URLs at the primary versions, so search engines consolidate to one copy.
- **2026-09-05 — Rendering defects repaired.** Broken table formatting, stray characters, and malformed headings introduced by a content-generation tool were fixed across roughly twenty pages, including comparison tables on the backup-vs-generator, financing, lease-vs-buy, and ground-mount pages.
