Every number a solar plan needs can be computed with a hand calculator and patience — these tools just do it faster, and each one shows its formulas so you can check the math yourself. Plan in this order: know your load, know your sun, then size hardware, then run the money.

## Interactive calculators

- [Solar Panel Angle Calculator](/pages/solar-panel-angle-calculator.html) — enter your latitude, get fixed and seasonal tilt angles, with the formulas explained and a table for common US cities
- [Solar Payback Calculator](/pages/solar-payback-calculator.html) — estimate payback period, 25-year savings, and net present value from system cost, production, electricity rate, and incentives

## Step-by-step worksheets

- [How to Calculate Your Solar Load](/pages/how-to-calculate-solar-load.html) — the daily watt-hour budget every other decision depends on, worked step by step with common appliance figures
- [The System Planner](/system-planner.html) — the full planning sequence: usage → panels → batteries → voltage → inverter → wiring → protection, in the order that avoids rework

## Where the numbers go next

Once the tools have given you panel watts, battery Wh, and payback years, the budget side takes over: the [solar system cost breakdown](/pages/solar-system-costs.html) prices those numbers line by line (installed and DIY), the [state cost guides](/guides.html) localize them, and [solar lease vs buy](/pages/solar-lease-vs-buy-2026.html) settles how to pay for it.

Load and sun-hours feed directly into [system sizing](/pages/solar-system-sizing.html); wiring decisions flow into [wire size](/pages/solar-wire-size.html) and [fuse sizing](/pages/solar-fuse-and-breaker-sizing.html); and the whole budget-first approach is framed in [Rebuilding Power From Scratch](/grid-down/rebuilding-power-from-scratch.html).
