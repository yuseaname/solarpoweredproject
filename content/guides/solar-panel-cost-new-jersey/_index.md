# How Much Do Solar Panels Cost in New Jersey in 2026?

As of mid-2026, the average solar panel cost in New Jersey is **$2.80 – $3.20 per watt installed**. A typical **8–9 kW** residential system costs roughly **$22,400 – $28,800**. The 30% federal ITC expired December 31, 2025 — but New Jersey answers with one of the best remaining stacks in the country: full-retail net metering, SREC-II (SuSI) certificate income paid per MWh for 15 years, and a sales tax exemption.

New Jersey homeowners pay an average residential electricity rate near **$0.18/kWh** and see **4.0 – 4.7 peak sun hours per day**. With SREC-II income included, simple payback typically runs **8–12 years** — among the best post-credit states.

## New Jersey Solar Incentives and Financial Benefits

- **SREC-II / SuSI program:** qualifying residential systems earn Solar Renewable Energy Certificates sold to the state at a set price per MWh generated, typically for 15 years — a second income stream on top of bill savings. Confirm current eligibility and enrollment windows with your installer.
- **Full-retail net metering:** New Jersey law credits exports at the retail rate with annual true-up — among the strongest protections in the nation.
- **Sales tax exemption:** solar equipment is exempt from the 6.625% state sales tax.
- **Property tax:** renewable systems are not assessed toward property tax increases.

With the federal credit gone, SREC-II income is what keeps New Jersey solar economics strong — treat it as the deciding factor in your quote comparison.

## Payback Period and Long-Term Savings

An 8.5 kW system generating ~11,500 kWh/year at $0.18/kWh avoids roughly **$2,070/year** in electricity. Add SREC-II income (per-MWh payments over 15 years) and effective simple payback lands around **8–12 years** for 2026 purchases. Over 25 years, cumulative savings plus certificate income can exceed **$60,000** before rate escalation.

## Is Solar Worth It in New Jersey in 2026?

For most homeowners with a sound roof, **yes — New Jersey is one of the strongest states post-credit**. Full-retail net metering plus SREC-II income replaces most of the lost federal subsidy. The key homework: confirm SREC-II availability and pricing at quote time, since program capacity is administered in tranches and terms evolve.

## FAQ

{{< faq "Does New Jersey still have full net metering in 2026?" >}}
Yes — New Jersey credits residential solar exports at the full retail rate with annual true-up. Combined with SREC-II income, this is among the best compensation structures in the country.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "What are SREC-IIs and how much do they pay?" >}}
Solar Renewable Energy Certificates represent the environmental value of your generation. Under New Jersey's SuSI program, qualifying systems sell SREC-IIs to the state at a set price per MWh for 15 years — roughly a few hundred dollars per year for a typical home system. Confirm current pricing and eligibility when you get quotes.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Is the federal tax credit available in New Jersey in 2026?" >}}
No. The 30% federal credit expired December 31, 2025 for new installs (2025 systems claim it on that year's return). New Jersey has no separate state income credit, but SREC-II income plus net metering and the sales tax exemption fill much of the gap.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "How does New Jersey solar compare to neighboring states?" >}}
Favorably. New York relies on upfront NY-Sun rebates and a 25% state credit; Pennsylvania has net metering but no certificate income at this scale. New Jersey's 15-year SREC-II stream plus retail-rate netting is arguably the strongest regional stack.
{{< /faq >}}
