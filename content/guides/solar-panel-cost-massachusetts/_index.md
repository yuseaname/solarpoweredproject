# How Much Do Solar Panels Cost in Massachusetts in 2026?

As of mid-2026, the average solar panel cost in Massachusetts is **$2.90 – $3.40 per watt installed** — among the higher-cost states. A typical **8–9 kW** residential system costs roughly **$23,200 – $30,600**. The 30% federal ITC expired December 31, 2025, but Massachusetts softens the blow better than most states: the SMART production program pays for every kWh your system generates, the state adds a 15% tax credit (capped at $1,000), and net metering is available for most residential systems.

Massachusetts homeowners pay some of the highest electricity rates in the country — at **29.61¢/kWh** ([EIA Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.A, June 2026](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/monthly/epm_table_grapher.php?t=epmt_5_6_a), retrieved 2026-09-05) — with **3.5–4.5 peak sun hours per day**. High rates offset modest sun; with SMART income included, simple payback typically runs **9–13 years**.

## Massachusetts Solar Incentives and Financial Benefits

- **SMART (Solar Massachusetts Renewable Target):** production-based payments for every kWh generated — value depends on your utility's current block and capacity factor. Verify the active block rate with your installer; blocks fill and rates step down.
- **Massachusetts residential energy credit:** 15% of system cost against state income tax, capped at $1,000.
- **Net metering:** available at near-retail credit for most residential systems through utility tariffs; Class I systems under 10 kW are generally exempt from certain charges.
- **Sales tax exemption:** solar equipment is exempt from the state's 6.25% sales tax.

With the federal credit gone, SMART income is Massachusetts's main lever — it behaves like a second revenue stream on top of bill savings.

## Payback Period and Long-Term Savings

An 8 kW system generating ~10,000 kWh/year at 29.61¢/kWh avoids roughly **$2,960/year** in electricity. Add SMART payments (block-rate dependent) and the effective payback lands around **9–13 years** — competitive with sunnier states despite New England weather, purely because rates are so high. Over 25 years, cumulative savings plus SMART income can exceed **$70,000** before utility rate escalation.

## Is Solar Worth It in Massachusetts in 2026?

For most homeowners with a suitable roof, **yes — Massachusetts is one of the better post-credit states**. The combination of ~30¢/kWh rates, SMART production income, the state credit, and net metering replaces much of the lost federal subsidy. Snow load and shading matter more here than in most states — favor south-facing, unshaded roof planes and confirm SMART block availability before signing.

## FAQ

{{< faq "Does Massachusetts still have net metering in 2026?" >}}
Yes — most residential systems under 10 kW receive near-retail credit for exports through utility tariffs, with SMART payments layered on top. Larger systems face caps and different rules; confirm with your utility.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "What is the SMART program and how much does it pay?" >}}
SMART pays a fixed rate for every kWh your system produces (not just exports) for 10 years. The rate depends on your utility's current capacity block and steps down as blocks fill — your installer can quote the exact current rate.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Is the federal tax credit available in Massachusetts in 2026?" >}}
No. The 30% federal credit expired December 31, 2025 for new installs. Systems placed in service in 2025 still claim it on that year's return. Massachusetts's own 15% state credit (max $1,000) still applies.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Do solar panels work well in Massachusetts winters?" >}}
Production drops 30–50% in winter months, but annual output is still solid — panels actually perform efficiently in cold temperatures. Snow typically slides off tilted panels within days; the bigger planning factors are roof orientation and tree shading.
{{< /faq >}}
