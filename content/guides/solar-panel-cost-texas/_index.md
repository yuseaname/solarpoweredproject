# How Much Do Solar Panels Cost in Texas in 2026?

As of mid-2026, the average solar panel cost in Texas is **$2.20 to $2.50 per watt installed** before incentives, making Texas one of the most affordable states for residential solar. A typical 11–12.5 kW system sized for Texas cooling loads costs roughly **$24,000 to $31,000 before incentives**. After the 30% federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC), the net price drops to about **$16,800 to $21,700**.

Texas combines low installation costs with strong sun and high summer air-conditioning demand, which shortens payback periods compared to many other states. However, the state has **no mandatory statewide net metering**, so the value of exported solar depends on your retail electricity provider or utility.

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cost Per Watt** | $2.50 – $3.10 | $2.20 – $2.50 | 10–20% lower |
| **Typical System Cost** | $21,000 – $28,000 | $24,000 – $31,000 | Larger systems common |
| **Avg. Electricity Rate** | ~$0.18/kWh | ~$0.14/kWh | Lower baseline |
| **Avg. Sun Hours/Day** | 4.5 hours | 5.5+ hours | Higher production |

Texas installations are cheaper per watt, but systems are often larger because summer AC loads push homeowners toward 11–13 kW arrays. The result is a competitive payback despite lower electricity rates than California.

### Cost Per Watt Analysis

Marketplace data from EnergySage and other aggregators show Texas clustering near **$2.23/W** installed, with competitive bids as low as **$1.90/W** and premium quotes near **$2.80/W**. The spread reflects:

- Roof complexity and accessibility
- Panel efficiency and brand tier
- Whether the quote includes battery storage
- Local installer competition

For a 12 kW system at $2.30/W, the gross cost is **$27,600** before incentives.

## Factors Influencing Texas Solar Installation Prices

### Roof Type and Summer Heat

Tile roofs, steep pitches, and multi-level homes increase labor costs. High ambient temperatures also favor panels with low temperature coefficients, which can add a small premium but protect summer output.

### Deregulated Electricity Market

Texas retail providers set their own solar buyback rates. Some offer 1:1 net metering-style credits; others pay avoided-cost rates. A battery becomes valuable when export compensation is low.

### Local Installer Competition

Houston, Dallas-Fort Worth, Austin, and San Antonio have dense installer markets, which drives prices down. Rural areas may pay slightly more due to travel and fewer bids.

## Texas Solar Incentives and Financial Benefits

### Federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC)

The 30% federal solar tax credit applies to Texas systems in 2026. On a $27,600 system, that is **$8,280** in credit against federal tax liability.

### Property Tax Exemption

Texas offers a **100% property tax exemption** on the added value of solar equipment under Tax Code §11.27. Your home value may rise, but the solar portion is not taxed.

### Local Utility Rebates and Buyback Programs

- **Austin Energy:** $2,500 residential solar rebate (subject to funding)
- **CPS Energy (San Antonio):** solar programs and rebates for eligible customers
- **Oncor and some co-ops:** periodic incentive programs; check current availability
- **Retail providers:** some offer solar buyback plans; read the fine print on rate structures and true-up periods

## Payback Period and Long-Term Savings

A 10 kW system in Texas producing roughly 15,000 kWh/year at $0.14/kWh saves about **$2,100/year** on electricity. With a net cost near **$17,000**, simple payback is about **8 years**. Over 25 years, cumulative savings can exceed **$40,000–$50,000** before accounting for rising utility rates.

Adding a battery can improve economics if your utility charges time-of-use rates or offers low export compensation. See [solar battery cost 2026](../solar-battery-cost-2026/) for battery pricing.

## Is Solar Worth It in Texas in 2026?

Yes, for most homeowners with good sun exposure and a roof in reasonable condition. The combination of low cost per watt, strong peak sun hours, federal tax credit, and property tax exemption makes solar a solid long-term investment. The main variable to check is your specific **buyback or net metering arrangement**, which determines how valuable exported solar is.
