# How Much Do Solar Panels Cost in Illinois in 2026?

As of mid-2026, the average solar panel cost in Illinois is **$2.70 – $3.10 per watt installed**. A typical **8–10 kW** residential system runs roughly **$21,600 – $31,000** before incentives. The 30% federal ITC expired December 31, 2025 — there is no federal credit on a 2026 install — but Illinois Shines, the state's renewable energy credit (REC) purchase program, still pays real value for your system's environmental attributes upfront through an approved installer.

Illinois homeowners pay an average residential electricity rate of **19.89¢/kWh** (EIA, June 2026) — above the 18.34¢ U.S. average — and see **4.0 – 4.5 peak sun hours per day**. Depending on how much REC value your quote carries, simple payback typically lands **9–13 years** for a 2026 cash purchase.

## Key takeaways

- **$2.70–$3.10/W installed** in Illinois in 2026; an 8 kW system runs **$21,600–$24,800** gross.
- **No federal credit for 2026 installs.** The 30% ITC expired Dec 31, 2025; systems installed in 2025 claim it on that year's return.
- **Illinois residential electricity averaged 19.89¢/kWh in June 2026** — above the national average — and the rate does more work in your payback math than Illinois' modest sun hours do.
- **Illinois Shines (the Adjustable Block Program)** buys 15 years of your system's RECs through an Approved Vendor. It is the state's main incentive, and its value is quote-dependent.
- **Retail-rate net metering survives** in ComEd and Ameren territory — confirm your utility's current distributed-generation rider before signing.

## What's different in Illinois

Illinois is one of the few states where the state incentive is not a tax credit you claim in April — it is a **purchase of your RECs**, structured into your installer contract. That changes how you shop.

- **Illinois Shines (Adjustable Block Program).** The state-administered program, run by the Illinois Power Agency, has utilities buy the RECs from qualified distributed-generation and community solar projects; the payment goes to your Approved Vendor and may be passed to you as savings, typically as an upfront discount on the contract price ([illinoisshines.com](https://www.illinoisshines.com/), retrieved 2026-09-05). Capacity is released in blocks, so the REC price on your quote depends on which block is open when you sign. Only an Approved Vendor in good standing can deliver this value — ask for their ABP status before signing.
- **Net metering at the retail rate.** ComEd and Ameren credit residential exports at essentially the retail rate (with a small supplementary charge on the credit) under their distributed-generation riders. Our <a href="/pages/net-metering-by-state-2026.html" class="text-link">net metering by state guide</a> lists Illinois as full retail credit plus REC value. Riders change — confirm the current terms with your utility.
- **Property tax protection.** Residential solar is assessed via a special assessment so it does not inflate your property tax bill — confirm the current treatment with your county assessor.
- **No state income tax credit.** Illinois deliberately routes its incentive through the REC market instead. If a salesperson pitches a "state tax credit," that's a red flag.

The practical consequence: in most states you compare quotes on price per watt; in Illinois you compare quotes on **price per watt *minus* the REC line**. Two bids $2,000 apart can flip order once REC value is broken out. Insist on seeing it itemized, and verify it against the program's current block rate.

## Payback period at Illinois' verified electricity rate

Illinois' above-average retail rate is the quiet engine of this math. Illinois residential electricity averaged **19.89¢/kWh in June 2026** (U.S. average: 18.34¢) — Source: EIA, Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.A, https://www.eia.gov/electricity/monthly/epm_table_grapher.php?t=epmt_5_6_a, retrieved 2026-09-05.

**Worked example — 8 kW in ComEd territory.** Illinois arrays produce roughly 1,300–1,400 kWh per installed kW per year at 4.0–4.5 sun hours. An 8 kW system: 8 × 1,300–1,400 = **10,400–11,200 kWh/year**. At 19.89¢/kWh: 10,400 × $0.1989 ≈ $2,070 and 11,200 × $0.1989 ≈ $2,230 — call it **$2,100–$2,200/year** in avoided bills before export-credit charges. Gross cost of $21,600–$24,800 ÷ $2,100–$2,200 = **10–11.5 years** simple payback with *zero* REC value. Apply a typical Illinois Shines REC discount — often 25–30% of system value, program-dependent; verify the actual line on your quote — and effective payback shortens to roughly **7–9 years**. The realistic planning band for a 2026 purchase is **9–13 years**, set almost entirely by your block rate and vendor.

| System | Gross cost | Annual kWh offset | Annual savings @ 19.89¢/kWh | Payback, no REC | Payback, 25–30% REC discount |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 6 kW | $16,200 – $18,600 | 7,800 – 8,400 | $1,550 – $1,670 | 10 – 12 yrs | 7 – 9 yrs |
| 8 kW | $21,600 – $24,800 | 10,400 – 11,200 | $2,070 – $2,230 | 10 – 11.5 yrs | 7 – 9 yrs |

*Assumes ~1,300–1,400 kWh per kW per year, self-consumption plus retail-rate export credit, and no rate escalation. Your utility's rider terms and your actual usage pattern move these numbers — treat the table as a frame, not a quote.*

Over 25 years, an 8 kW system at this rate offsets roughly **$52,000–$55,000** in electricity before rate escalation and panel degradation. Run your own numbers with the <a href="/pages/solar-payback-calculator.html" class="text-link">solar payback calculator</a>.

## Is solar worth it in Illinois in 2026?

For most homeowners, **yes — if the Illinois Shines discount is properly applied**. The REC program does much of what the federal credit used to do, retail-rate export credit survives, and the verified 19.89¢/kWh rate means each kilowatt-hour your array offsets is worth more than in most of the country. The risks are program-driven, not sun-driven: block capacity and vendor pricing vary, so compare at least three Approved Vendors and verify each quote's REC math against the program's current block rates. If a quote arrives with the REC value bundled and unexplained, walk — the discount is your leverage.

## FAQ

{{< faq "Does Illinois still have net metering in 2026?" >}}
Yes — ComEd and Ameren credit residential solar exports at essentially the retail rate under their distributed-generation riders, with annual true-up. Confirm the exact rider and any credit-related charges with your utility.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "What is Illinois Shines and how much is it worth?" >}}
Illinois Shines is the state's renewable energy credit purchase program. Approved vendors sell your system's 15 years of RECs to the state and pass the value back as an upfront discount — often a meaningful share of system cost. Block rates change; the number on your quote should cite the current block.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Is the federal tax credit available in Illinois in 2026?" >}}
No. The 30% federal credit expired December 31, 2025 for new installs (2025 systems claim it on that year's return). Illinois has no separate state income credit — the REC program is the state's incentive vehicle.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Do I have to use an approved Illinois Shines vendor?" >}}
Yes, to receive REC value the installation must go through an approved vendor in good standing. Ask for the vendor's ABP status and block allocation before signing — unapproved installs forfeit the state incentive entirely.
{{< /faq >}}
