# How Much Do Solar Panels Cost in Illinois in 2026?

As of mid-2026, the average solar panel cost in Illinois is **$2.70 – $3.10 per watt installed**. A typical **8–10 kW** residential system costs roughly **$21,600 – $31,000**. The 30% federal ITC expired December 31, 2025 — but Illinois Shines (the state's Adjustable Block program) pays significant value for your system's renewable energy certificates upfront, partially filling the gap.

Illinois homeowners pay an average residential electricity rate near **$0.16/kWh** and see **4.0 – 4.5 peak sun hours per day**. With Illinois Shines REC value applied, simple payback typically runs **9–13 years** for 2026 purchases.

## Illinois Solar Incentives and Financial Benefits

- **Illinois Shines (Adjustable Block Program):** the state buys your system's RECs — for 15 years of environmental attributes — delivering a substantial upfront or financed discount through approved vendors. This is the biggest remaining lever in Illinois; only work with approved installers.
- **Net metering:** ComEd and Ameren credit residential exports at the retail rate (with a small supplementary charge on the credit); distributed generation rides under state rules — confirm your utility's current rider.
- **Special assessment for property tax:** solar adds no property tax burden on residential systems.
- **No state income credit:** Illinois relies on the REC structure instead.

With the federal credit gone, Illinois Shines REC value is the make-or-break line item — insist on seeing it broken out in every quote.

## Payback Period and Long-Term Savings

A 9 kW system generating ~12,500 kWh/year at $0.16/kWh avoids roughly **$2,000/year** in electricity. Apply the Illinois Shines REC discount (often 25–30% of system value, program-dependent) and effective simple payback lands **9–13 years** for 2026 purchases. Over 25 years, cumulative savings can exceed **$50,000** before rate escalation.

## Is Solar Worth It in Illinois in 2026?

For most homeowners, **yes — if the Illinois Shines discount is properly applied**. The REC program does much of what the federal credit used to do, and retail-rate export credit survives. The risks are program-driven: block capacity and vendor pricing vary, so compare at least three approved vendors and verify each quote's REC math against the program's current block rates.

## FAQ

{{< faq "Does Illinois still have net metering in 2026?" >}}
Yes — ComEd and Ameren credit residential solar exports at essentially the retail rate under their distributed-generation riders, with annual true-up. Confirm the exact rider and any credit-related charges with your utility.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "What is Illinois Shines and how much is it worth?" >}}
Illinois Shines is the state's renewable energy credit purchase program. Approved vendors sell your system's 15 years of RECs to the state and pass the value back as an upfront discount — often a meaningful share of system cost. Block rates change; the number on your quote should cite the current block.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Is the federal tax credit available in Illinois in 2026?" >}}
No. The 30% federal credit expired December 31, 2025 for new installs (2025 systems claim it on that year's return). Illinois has no separate state income credit — the REC program is the state's incentive vehicle.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Do I have to use an approved Illinois Shines vendor?" >}}
Yes, to receive REC value the installation must go through an approved vendor in good standing. Ask for the vendor's ABP status and block allocation before signing — unapproved installs forfeit the state incentive entirely.
{{< /faq >}}
