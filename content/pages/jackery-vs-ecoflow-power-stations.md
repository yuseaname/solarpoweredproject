## Quick answer

The Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 and EcoFlow DELTA 2 are the same class — ~1kWh LiFePO4 solar generators — and the spec sheet is closer than the brands' marketing suggests: **1,070Wh vs 1,024Wh capacity (a 4.5% difference that never decides anything), 1,500W vs 1,800W continuous AC, ~400W vs ~500W solar input** (all per manufacturer specifications, retrieved 2026-09-06). What actually decides it: **your biggest continuous load against the 1,500W/1,800W line** (a microwave, hair dryer, or job-site tool settles it), whether you'll **expand later** (the DELTA 2 takes extra batteries; the Jackery doesn't), and whether you carry it (the Jackery is ~2.6 lbs lighter). Neither is "better" — one of them fits your load list, and this page shows you which.

We test nothing. Every number below is manufacturer-published and dated; the comparisons are arithmetic you can re-run.

## The gate math: your biggest continuous load

This single calculation picks the winner more often than every other spec combined:

- **Loads under 1,500W** (fridge, CPAP, router+laptop, lights, TV, fan, most medical devices): both units run everything you have — the gate doesn't decide, move to the tiebreakers below.
- **Loads between 1,500W and 1,800W** (many hair dryers, some microwaves, some induction plates, job-site tools): the DELTA 2 runs them; the Jackery 1000 v2 doesn't. If your emergency list includes one of these, that's the ballgame — or consider the Jackery 2000-class instead of fighting the gate.
- **Loads over 1,800W** (well pumps that turn out to be 240V submersibles, big table saws, some space heaters): neither unit — and no adapter fixes a 240V submersible; that's the [well-pump page's](/pages/solar-generator-well-pump-sizing.html) separate problem.

Worked runtime math at the class level (usable ≈ 90% of nameplate on LiFePO4): 1,070 × 0.9 ≈ **963Wh** vs 1,024 × 0.9 ≈ **922Wh** — about 21 vs 20 hours of a 45W CPAP+humidifier setup, or roughly one day of a modern fridge's cycling (~0.9–1.2kWh/day; measure yours with the method in [our refrigerator sizing page](/pages/what-size-solar-generator-run-refrigerator.html)). A 4% capacity difference disappears into measurement noise.

## Solar recharge: where they actually differ

With their max solar inputs (400W vs 500W, per manufacturer specs) and ~85% charge efficiency:

- **DELTA 2 at 500W in good sun:** 0 → 100% in roughly 2.5–3 sun-hours.
- **Jackery at 400W:** roughly 3–3.5 sun-hours.
- **Both need the panels bought or bundled separately** — check whether the listing you're buying includes them (the EcoFlow 220W bundle is a common configuration).

In winter or shade, both numbers stretch badly (see [peak sun hours](/pages/peak-sun-hours-by-state.html)); if solar recharge is your primary plan, the 25% input difference matters more than any capacity difference. If you're mostly wall-charging between uses, ignore this row entirely.

## The tiebreakers that remain

1. **Expansion:** planning to grow into whole-outage coverage later? The DELTA 2's extra-battery path (~2–3kWh, per manufacturer) means the base unit isn't dead weight. The Jackery path is buying a second station.
2. **Weight and grab-ability:** ~23.8 vs ~26.4 lbs and the Jackery's smaller case — real for campers, irrelevant for a shelf unit by the freezer.
3. **Cycle-life conditions:** if you'll cycle daily for years, the end-of-life threshold (70% vs 80%) deserves a look — both are LiFePO4 multi-thousand-cycle chemistry, a generational improvement over the NMC units both brands sold five years ago.
4. **Outlets and pass-through:** count what your actual load list needs (the DELTA 2 carries more outlets; both pass through for UPS-style duty with fast switchover per manufacturer claims).

**And the third option worth naming:** at this budget, some readers are better served by a 100Ah LiFePO4 battery + inverter + controller built to the same capacity for less money — the [DIY vs station tradeoffs](/pages/solar-generator.html) are worked out in our solar generator guide. Stations win on integration and grab-and-go; builds win on $/Wh and growth.

## Frequently Asked Questions

{{< faq "Which is better overall, Jackery or EcoFlow?" >}}
Neither, honestly — they're the same class with one meaningful divergence: the DELTA 2's 1,800W continuous rating and expandability versus the Jackery's lighter case. Write down your biggest continuous load and whether you'll expand; that list picks, not the brand.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Is 1,070Wh vs 1,024Wh a real difference?" >}}
No — about 4.5%, or roughly 20 minutes of fridge runtime. Both round to "one fridge for a day, one CPAP for a night." Capacity marketing at the same class level is noise; the continuous-wattage gate is signal.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Can either one run a microwave?" >}}
Depends on the microwave: many run 1,000–1,500W (both stations handle), some draw 1,500–1,800W (DELTA 2 only), and surge on start matters less for microwaves than for motor loads. Check the appliance's plate, not its marketing.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Did you test these stations?" >}}
No. This comparison is built from manufacturer-published specifications (retrieved 2026-09-06) and arithmetic — capacity, gate math, recharge estimates. We run no test lab and accept no review units; how we pick products is documented in [how we recommend](/pages/how-we-recommend.html).
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Which charges faster with solar panels?" >}}
The DELTA 2's 500W input beats the Jackery's 400W by about 25% on paper, worth roughly half a sun-hour on a full recharge. If off-grid recharging is your main use, weight that row; if you wall-charge, it barely matters.
{{< /faq >}}
