## Quick answer: what a solar system costs in 2026

A grid-tied home system runs **$2.50–$3.50 per watt installed** — about **$15,000–$21,000 for a 6kW system** before any incentives. Buying the components and doing the work yourself typically lands **40–60% lower** in parts alone. And the number that shaped solar budgets for a decade is gone: the 30% federal ITC expired December 31, 2025, so 2026 buyers pay the full hardware price unless a state or utility incentive applies.

## Key takeaways

-   Panels are usually the largest hardware line, but labor and soft costs are the largest *overall* share on an installed quote — hardware is only 40–50% of what installers charge.
-   Batteries can double total cost for off-grid systems; on installed quotes they add roughly $1,000–$1,400 per kWh.
-   Balance-of-system parts (racking, wiring, breakers, combiners) add 15–25% to a DIY build and are the line item most first-time budgets forget.
-   There is no federal tax credit for 2026 installs. Payback now runs roughly 10–13 years in high-cost, high-rate states; state and utility incentives still exist.

## What changed in 2026

**The federal credit is gone.** The 30% residential ITC expired December 31, 2025 under P.L. 119-21. Systems installed in 2025 can still claim it on that year's return, but a 2026 installation gets no federal credit — budget the full price. On a $21,000 installed system, that is $6,300 that no longer comes back at tax time.

**Payback stretched by roughly a third.** Before the expiry, a typical credit-era install paid back in 7–10 years. In 2026, the realistic national band is 10–14 years, and high-cost, high-rate states like California land around 10–13 years for cash purchases. The math did not stop working — the annual savings are unchanged — but the break-even point moved out because the net cost went up.

**State incentives still exist.** State tax credits, utility rebates, and performance incentives (SRECs in some markets) were never affected by the federal expiry. California's SGIP remains among the strongest for battery storage. Check your state's programs before you size the budget — a meaningful state rebate can claw back several years of payback.

## What changes the price?

System size, battery choice, inverter type, mounting complexity, and local labor rates all affect totals. Three matter more than the rest:

-   **Battery or no battery.** On off-grid builds, storage routinely costs more than the panels. On grid-tied builds, skipping the battery is the single biggest cost saving available.
-   **Roof complexity.** Steep pitches, dormers, and panel upgrades (a main panel upgrade alone can add $2,000–$4,000) show up directly in the labor line.
-   **Where you buy the panels.** The spread between one-off 100W modules at $1.00/W and pallet pricing at $0.45/W is thousands of dollars on a 6kW array.

## FAQ

{{< faq "How much would a 6kW system cost DIY vs installed?" >}}
DIY parts run $5,100–$9,600: panels $2,700–$4,200, string inverter $1,000–$2,000, racking $600–$1,200, wiring and protection $500–$1,200, permits $300–$1,000. The same system installed runs $15,000–$21,000 at the 2026 rate of $2.50–$3.50/W. The difference is almost entirely labor, permitting handling, and installer margin — the hardware lists are nearly identical.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "How much does a 10kW system with a 10kWh battery cost in 2026?" >}}
Installed: $35,000–$48,000, of which the battery accounts for $10,000–$14,000 at installed rates. As a DIY parts build: $12,800–$25,500, with the battery at $4,000–$9,000 in components. The battery is the layer where DIY versus installed diverges most — component pricing is roughly a third of installed pricing for the same usable kWh.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Does the 30% federal tax credit still apply in 2026?" >}}
No. The residential ITC expired December 31, 2025 (P.L. 119-21). Systems installed in 2025 can still claim it on that year's return; 2026 installations get no federal credit. State tax credits, utility rebates, and storage incentives like California's SGIP are separate programs and still available.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Is DIY solar cheaper than an installed system?" >}}
Material-for-material, yes — often 40–60% less, because installed quotes bundle labor, permitting, and margin. You are trading that saving for your own design, code, and warranty work, plus the interconnection paperwork with your utility. On a 6kW build that trade is worth roughly $9,000–$11,000.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "What is the biggest cost in a solar system?" >}}
For grid-tied installs, labor and soft costs — hardware is only 40–50% of an installed quote. For off-grid builds, storage: batteries routinely cost more than the panels they support, which is why battery math dominates DIY budgets.
{{< /faq >}}
