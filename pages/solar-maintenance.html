Maintenance

Solar maintenance checklist

Routine maintenance keeps output consistent and helps you spot issues early.

Informational only. Follow manufacturer guidance and local code requirements.

Key takeaways

  • Track production monthly and compare to expected output.
  • Inspect wiring, mounts, and inverter alerts twice per year.
  • Clean panels only when visible buildup is causing real losses.
  • Battery issues are common in off-grid systems; monitor voltage and charge trends.

Safety first

Solar systems involve high-voltage DC, AC circuits, and rooftop work. If you need to inspect wiring or open electrical enclosures, shut down the system following the manufacturer sequence and local code requirements. Use proper personal protective equipment and avoid working in wet conditions.

Do not work alone outdoors.

If you are unsure about electrical safety, consult a licensed electrician. Use the wiring decisions checklist to plan safe layouts.

Basic tools and supplies

Most routine checks require only a flashlight, soft brush, and a way to view system monitoring data. Avoid abrasive tools that can scratch panel glass. If you need to access roof-mounted panels, use appropriate fall protection or hire a professional.

Monthly monitoring checklist

  • Review inverter or monitoring app for alerts or error codes.
  • Compare daily or weekly kWh to the same season last year.
  • Check for new shading from trees, vents, or debris.
  • Confirm battery state of charge patterns (if applicable).

If output drops unexpectedly, start with the low output troubleshooting guide.

Seasonal inspection (2–4 times per year)

  • Inspect visible wiring for discoloration, cracks, or loose connectors.
  • Check mounts, rails, and roof penetrations for movement or corrosion.
  • Clear debris from around inverters and ensure ventilation is unobstructed.
  • Look for signs of water intrusion in conduits or junction boxes.

If you see hot cables, melting insulation, or a burnt smell, shut down the system and contact a professional.

Record keeping

Keep a simple log of monthly production, inverter alerts, and maintenance actions. A basic spreadsheet with dates and notes makes it easier to spot trends and confirm whether changes helped. Good records also help if you need warranty service or professional support.

Panel cleaning: when it matters

Many systems need little cleaning. Rain can remove light dust, so cleaning is most useful after heavy pollen, wildfire smoke, or long dry periods. Cleaning is worth it if you see visible buildup and a sustained drop in production during sunny weather.

Clean early in the morning or on a cool day. Use soft water and non-abrasive tools. Avoid harsh chemicals that can damage coatings. For cost estimates, see solar panel cleaning cost.

Electrical checks (visual only)

Look for loose connectors, cracked insulation, and signs of heat. Verify that wire runs are supported and protected from sharp edges. If you see corrosion or water intrusion, address it quickly before it damages equipment.

Do not disconnect or modify wiring unless you are qualified. High-voltage DC can arc and cause injury.

After storms or unusual weather

High winds, hail, or heavy snow can shift mounts, damage wiring, or crack panels. After severe weather, do a visual inspection from the ground, then check production data for sudden drops. If you see obvious damage, contact a professional before attempting repairs.

Inverter and charge controller checks

Inverters and controllers log warnings when input voltage, temperature, or loads are outside normal range. Review the logs monthly. Repeated errors often point to overloads, poor ventilation, or battery issues.

Grid-tied vs off-grid maintenance

Grid-tied systems focus on panel and inverter health because the grid provides backup. Off-grid systems require more attention to batteries and charge controllers because storage must cover nights and poor weather. Off-grid owners should track battery voltage and state of charge more closely.

Battery maintenance (off-grid systems)

Lead-acid batteries may require periodic water checks and equalization cycles. Lithium batteries rely on a battery management system (BMS) and should be kept within recommended temperature ranges. If batteries are consistently undercharged, they will age faster.

Use the battery capacity calculator to confirm whether your bank still matches your loads. For chemistry comparison, see li-ion vs lead-acid.

Performance baseline

Establish a baseline by tracking average daily kWh during a stable month. This makes it easier to spot meaningful changes. If your system suddenly underperforms, compare weather conditions, shading, and inverter status before assuming a hardware failure.

The panel output calculator helps compare expected production with actual data.

Maintenance schedule at a glance

  • Monthly: check monitoring data, shading changes, and alerts.
  • Seasonal: inspect mounts, wiring, and inverter ventilation.
  • Annual: review overall performance trends and update your baseline.

Adjust the schedule for harsh climates, heavy dust, or systems with aging batteries.

Maintenance record tip

Keep a simple log of inspections and output changes. It helps spot slow declines early.

Simple troubleshooting flow

  1. Check the weather and recent sun hours.
  2. Review inverter or controller alerts.
  3. Inspect for shading or debris on panels.
  4. Compare output to last month or last year.
  5. Only then check wiring or battery issues.

This sequence prevents unnecessary tinkering and helps you focus on the most likely causes first.

Wiring and connection warning signs

Loose or corroded connections can cause heat, voltage drop, and intermittent output. Signs include warm connectors, discoloration, or flickering inverter status. If you see these, shut down the system and consult a professional rather than tightening live connections.

Micro-inverter vs string maintenance

Micro-inverters allow panel-level monitoring, which makes it easier to spot a single underperforming panel. String inverters are simpler, but a single shaded or failed panel can affect the whole string. Use your monitoring data to spot these patterns.

Common maintenance issues

  • Shading changes from new growth or nearby construction.
  • Loose or corroded connectors causing intermittent output drops.
  • Inverter overheating due to blocked airflow.
  • Battery bank drifting out of balance or low capacity.

Warranty and documentation

Keep datasheets, invoices, and warranty terms in one place. If you need support, manufacturers often ask for model numbers, installation dates, and monitoring logs. Good documentation speeds up troubleshooting and warranty claims.

Visible warning signs

From the ground, look for cracked glass, sagging mounts, or missing fasteners. Inside the equipment area, watch for corrosion, pest damage, or water stains. These issues can turn into electrical faults if left unaddressed.

If you are unsure, photograph the issue and compare with earlier photos. Small changes are easier to fix early. Keep a yearly photo set for reference to save time and money.

When to call a professional

Contact a licensed electrician or installer if you see arcing, repeated inverter shutdowns, damaged wiring, or any issue involving the service panel or grid interconnection. If you are unsure about code compliance or safety, professional help is the safest option.

FAQ

How often should I clean my panels?

Only when visible dirt or debris is causing measurable output drops.

Why does output drop in winter?

Shorter days, lower sun angles, and snow cover reduce production.

Do batteries need maintenance?

Lead-acid batteries often do; lithium batteries need temperature and charge management.

What if my inverter shows an error code?

Check the manual first, then troubleshoot loads, wiring, and battery voltage.
