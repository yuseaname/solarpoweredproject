# Amazon Associates US rules — verification memo (retrieved 2026-09-05)

**Method:** Primary sources fetched directly this session: Associates Program Operating Agreement (`https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/agreement`, page states "Updated: October 15, 2025"), Program Policies page (`https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/policies`, serves the Participation Requirements + Commission Income Statement), OA change log (`https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/compare`), FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ (`https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking`). All quotes below are verbatim from fetched text. All retrieved 2026-09-05.

## 1. Disclosure placement/wording
- **FACT (OA §5):** "You must clearly and prominently state the following… on your Site or any other location where Amazon may authorize your display or other use of Program Content: **'As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.'**" The OA states the required statement at site/location level; it does not itself mandate per-link placement. A variant claiming Amazon requires the notice "on each page offering products" — **NEEDS VERIFICATION** in Associates help pages (login-walled).
- **FACT (FTC FAQ):** Disclosure must be "clearly and conspicuously" placed; "The closer the disclosure is to your recommendation, the better." A single disclosure may suffice when "the reader can see both the review containing that disclosure and the link at the same time. But if the product review containing the disclosure and the link are separated, readers may not make the connection." FTC example wording: "I get commissions for purchases made through links in this post."
- **FACT (FTC FAQ):** "affiliate link" alone is **not** adequate; a "buy now" button is not; "commissionable link" is not; "**paid link** right next to an affiliate link should be an adequate disclosure."
- **INFERENCE (combined standard):** For solarpoweredproject.com, use the exact Amazon sentence sitewide (footer + a disclosure block near top of monetized pages) **plus** a short FTC-style line adjacent to link clusters/tables. This satisfies both without conflict.

## 2. Pricing rule — hardcoded prices
- **FACT (Policies/Participation Requirements):** "Because prices for and availability of Products that you have listed on your Site may change, your Site may only show prices and availability if: (a) we serve the link in which that price and availability data are displayed, or (b) you obtain Product pricing and availability data via Creators API or PA API and you comply with the requirements regarding use of Creators API or PA API in the License."
- **FACT:** In comparison formats you "must display both the lowest 'new' price and, if we provide it to you, the lowest 'used' price" on the Amazon Site; and a disclaimer is required "adjacent to the pricing or availability information or… via a hyperlink, pop-up box, scripted pop-up": "Product prices and availability are accurate as of the date/time indicated and are subject to change. Any price and availability information displayed on [relevant Amazon Site(s), as applicable] at the time of purchase will apply to the purchase of this product."
- **FACT:** "Unless otherwise agreed by Amazon, your Site must not have price tracking and/or price alerting functionality."
- **INFERENCE (strategy):** Hardcoded price tables are non-compliant unless Amazon-served (e.g., native shopping ads/widgets) or PA API-fed with the required disclaimer. Hugo static site: either integrate PA API (prices are then served live per API license terms; PA API images/cache may be stored only per license — "you may store a link to Product Advertising Content consisting of an image for up to 24 hours" — FACT) or avoid prices entirely and use specs/runtime math (which fits the site's honest-math positioning). Static hardcoded "$" cells in comparison tables = compliance risk.

## 3. Cookie/session window (strategy-relevant)
- **FACT (Commission Income Statement):** Qualifying purchases require a click through a Special Link, then a "single session… ending upon the first to occur of… (x) 24 hours elapse from that click, (y) the customer places an order…"; items added to cart must be ordered "no later than 89 days after their initial click-through"; and, under a change shown on the compare page, "products must be shipped to, streamed or downloaded by, and paid for by the customer" within 180 days to qualify.
- **INFERENCE (strategy):** Content that drives a click before the reader is decision-ready wastes the window; decision/troubleshooting pages (the site's winners) should place links at the "what to buy" moment. Also FACT (compare page): purchases "referred through any paid or boosted advertisement linking to Amazon" are disqualified — do not run paid traffic to monetized pages.

## 4. Link behavior
- **FACT (Policies):** "All Special Links must be accessed directly from your Site"; don't encourage bookmarking Special Links; you may not conceal the origin — no use of "Redirecting Links… or the user agent… such that we cannot reasonably determine the site or application from which a customer clicks" (the anti-cloaking/masking rule); no "rewards or loyalty program that incentivizes persons or entities to visit an Amazon Site via your Special Links"; no pop-up/pop-under/transition ads carrying Special Links; no Special Links placed on Amazon properties; no self-purchase for resale/commercial use.
- **FACT (OA §5 + Policies):** You "will not misrepresent or embellish our relationship… (including by expressing or implying that we support, sponsor, or endorse you)" — no "recommended by Amazon" claims. OA §6 treats any §5 violation as material breach permitting immediate termination.
- **FACT (Policies):** Offline/email prohibition: no Special Links "in any printed material, mailing, SMS, MMS, attachment to email, or other document, or any oral solicitation"; limited exception permits Special Links in "emails, SMS and direct messaging from your social media Sites; provided, that such communications are solicited (i.e., opted into by the receiving customer)."
- **INFERENCE:** Newsletter monetization requires opt-in list + compliance review before any Special Links go in email.

## 5. Review quality, "best" claims, AI
- **FACT (Policies):** "You must not make inaccurate, overbroad, deceptive or otherwise misleading claims about any Product, an Amazon Site, or any of our policies, promotions, or prices." No clause expressly naming "best" claims was found in fetched text; **NEEDS VERIFICATION** whether another help page adds one. FTC substantiation principles independently cover superlatives.
- **FACT (FTC FAQ, referencing the Guides "revised in 2023… including a new definition of 'clearly and conspicuously'"):** An endorsement "must reflect the honest opinion of the endorser"; "You can't talk about your experience with a product if you haven't tried it"; "If you've only tried a product once, you can't suggest you use it regularly"; an endorser may be liable for claims beyond their personal experience. Deception standard: misleading "a significant minority" of consumers.
- **AI-assisted content:** The FTC FAQ page I fetched contains no AI-specific Q&A (zero matches for "artificial intelligence"), and an FTC blog post on AI-generated fake reviews could not be retrieved (404 on the URL I attempted). **NEEDS VERIFICATION.** INFERENCE: under the 2023 Guides as fetched, AI-drafted review text must still reflect honest, substantiated opinion; fabricated "we tested" narratives would violate both the FTC honest-experience principle and Amazon's misleading-claims rule — consistent with the client's no-fake-testing ethics rule.
