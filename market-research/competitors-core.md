## 1. EnergySage — https://www.energysage.com/ (retrieved 2026-09-05)
- What/model: US solar marketplace connecting homeowners with "pre-screened,
  certified installers"; developed "with funding from the U.S. Department of
  Energy"; claims 60,000+ homeowners served (FACT: homepage). Lead-gen
  monetization: ZIP-code quote capture and "Get solar quotes" CTAs throughout
  (FACT: homepage); marketplace/lead-gen interpretation is INFERENCE.
- Content: homeowner-purchase guides (costs, financing, incentives; "Best solar
  batteries for your home in 2026," Emily Walker & Casey McDevitt, Apr 29, 2026)
  (FACT: homepage). Named editorial team, public editorial guidelines, "Energy
  Advisors," market-intel reports (FACT: homepage).
- Methodology: not visible from homepage fetch; battery picks are
  brand/system-level (INFERENCE).
- SEO breadth: very broad — solar, batteries, heat pumps, EV charging,
  electricity plans (FACT: homepage nav).
- Weaknesses to exploit: (1) zero DIY/off-grid/12V coverage in nav or homepage
  content — no charge controllers, cable sizing, or build guides (FACT: nav;
  INFERENCE: gap); (2) every path funnels to a ZIP-quote sale — readers wanting
  a straight spec-level answer without being sold installer contact get little
  (INFERENCE).

## 2. SolarReviews — https://www.solarreviews.com/ (retrieved 2026-09-05)
- What/model: consumer reviews of US installers + expert equipment reviews +
  savings calculator usable "without inputting any personal information"
  (FACT: homepage). Lead-gen CTAs ("Find trusted local solar companies") and
  installer phone lines (FACT: homepage + battery article).
- Trust: named leadership with solar-founder backgrounds (Andrew Sendy,
  Lachlan Fleet) and "50+ years combined" editorial experience (FACT: homepage,
  their claim); battery article bylined Ben Zientara, edited Catherine Lane
  (FACT: https://www.solarreviews.com/blog/best-solar-batteries).
- Methodology: strongest of the group — published weighted battery criteria
  (company financial strength 20%, value 15%, design 14%, warranty 10%,
  capacity 10%, power 10%, safety certs 10%) plus proprietary survey data
  ("74% of solar installers now install Enphase batteries," 2025 Solar Industry
  Survey) (FACT: battery article). Spec-sheet-plus-industry-data, not hands-on
  testing (INFERENCE).
- Weaknesses: (1) rankings optimize for whole-home installed systems and giant
  brands — a "financial strength" weighting is meaningless to a DIYer choosing
  a $150 charge controller; no DIY/12V cluster (INFERENCE from methodology +
  homepage scope); (2) freshness lag — flagship battery roundup is still the
  2025 edition ("Updated 1 year ago") while EnergySage ships 2026 editions
  (FACT: page label; INFERENCE: competitive lag).

## 3. Clean Energy Reviews — https://www.cleanenergyreviews.info/ (retrieved 2026-09-05)
- What/model: independent technical review site; deep guides on panels,
  hybrid/off-grid inverters, home batteries, V2G EV charging; own tools (string
  voltage calculator, off-grid load calculator) and a forum (FACT: homepage).
  Battery content is Australia-framed ("best solar batteries in Australia")
  (FACT: homepage). Monetization not visible on homepage; "Solar Installers"
  nav implies installer referrals (INFERENCE).
- Methodology: cites independent lab testing (PVEL) for panel reliability
  (FACT: homepage). Analysis-driven, not purchase-link-driven (INFERENCE).
- Weaknesses: (1) AU-first lens — US pricing, brands (e.g., EG4, Anker SOLIX),
  incentives, and NEC context appear thin from what the homepage exposes
  (INFERENCE); (2) high technical density plus slower update cadence in
  sections (2024–2025 dates visible) can lose beginner US readers (FACT:
  dates on homepage; INFERENCE: effect).

## 6. mobile-solarpower.com (Will Prowse) (retrieved 2026-09-05)
- What/model: "Will Prowse Approved" gear curation tied to his YouTube channel

  reader needing a neutral wide comparison or a code/state question gets

## Verdict: white space among the BIG players
The big players split into two camps that barely overlap. Lead-gen marketplaces
(EnergySage, SolarReviews) monetize installer quotes for whole-home grid-tied
systems; their "best battery" lists rank Tesla/Enphase-class systems using
financial-strength and installer-popularity criteria that are irrelevant to
someone buying a $200 charge controller or sizing a 100Ah battery. Hands-on DIY
creators (Footprint Hero, mobile-solarpower.com) prove the DIY/12V niche is
Amazon-monetizable but cover it thinly, one person at a time, with no
state-level, code-level, or appliance-runtime depth. Clean Energy Reviews has
the technical authority but an Australian lens; ClimateBiz has breadth but no
testing story. Nobody owns the spec-level decision layer for the US retail/DIY
buyer: honest runtime math ("will a 100Ah battery run a CPAP all night"),
fuse/cable/wire-gauge decisions with NEC references, charge-controller
selection math, appliance-by-appliance backup planning — delivered with
transparent "here's the arithmetic, here's what we don't know" methodology and
calculators. That is exactly where the client's only pages that ever earned
traffic already sit. The play: build out that layer with SolarReviews-style
published methodology and Footprint-Hero-style transparency about what was
actually bought or not — without ever pretending to lab-test gear we don't own.
