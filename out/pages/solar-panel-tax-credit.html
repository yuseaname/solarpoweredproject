What is Solar Panel Tax Credit?

Simple Explanation

The solar panel tax credit—formally the Residential Clean Energy Credit—is a federal tax incentive that rewards you for installing qualified renewable energy systems. In 2026, it covers 30% of your total system cost (panels, inverter, battery, labor, permits, and even rooftop mounting hardware).

Key Components

  • Coverage: 30% of system cost (through 2032)
  • Eligible systems: Solar PV, solar water heaters, geothermal heat pumps, fuel cells, and battery storage (with ≥3 kWh capacity)
  • Time limit: Systems placed in service between 2022–2032 qualify for 30%

Federal Tax Credit

The IRS’s Residential Clean Energy Credit (Form 5695) applies to your 2026 tax return (filed in 2027). To claim it:

  1. Ensure your installer provides a certification statement confirming system eligibility
  2. File Form 5695 with your federal return
  3. Apply the credit against your tax liability—or carry it forward

Important: The credit is non-refundable. If your tax bill is $5,000 and your credit is $7,500, you’ll only reduce your bill to $0—but you can carry the remaining $2,500 to future years.

  • Florida: Property tax exemption for solar (no added assessment)

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the solar tax credit still worth it in 2026?

Absolutely—especially if you’re in a high-electric-rate state like CA, TX, or FL. With 30% off your system cost plus falling hardware prices, the payback period is under 6 years for most homeowners. And since you can combine it with state incentives, your actual net cost may be even lower. The DOE confirms this credit remains the most effective federal incentive for residential solar.

How long does the credit last on my return?

You claim the credit in the tax year the system is placed in service (i.e., fully installed and inspected). If you install in December 2026, you claim it on your 2026 tax return (filed April 2027). If your credit exceeds your tax liability, the remainder carries forward for up to five years—so you can use it on your 2027, 2028, 2029, 2030, and 2031 returns.

What maintenance does a solar system require?

Very little:

  1. Panel cleaning: Every 1–2 years (more in dusty/dry climates)
  2. Inverter check: Monitor app alerts (replace after ~10–12 years)
  3. Annual inspection: Verify connections and wiring (most installers offer this)

Batteries need no user maintenance—but Enphase and Tesla include remote diagnostics to catch issues early.

Does battery storage qualify for the tax credit?

Yes—if the battery is charged 100% by solar and installed with your solar system. Starting in 2023, standalone batteries no longer qualify unless paired with solar. In 2026, batteries with ≥3 kWh capacity (like Enphase IQ Battery or Powerwall) are fully credit-eligible.

Can I claim the credit if I lease or have a power purchase agreement (PPA)?

No. Only system owners qualify. Leases and PPAs are structured so the installer or financier claims the credit—so you won’t see direct tax savings. However, you’ll still benefit from lower monthly energy bills. If you want the tax credit, buy your system outright or with a solar loan.
